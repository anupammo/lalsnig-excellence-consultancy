# 06 — Keyword research

**Version 2.0 · 24 August 2026** — rewritten after the repositioning from *conveyor belt consulting*
to **business excellence consulting**.

---

## 0. What the repositioning costs, and what to do about it

The site now leads with **business excellence consulting**. That is a deliberate commercial decision
and this document follows it. It is also a materially harder SEO position than the one it replaces,
and pretending otherwise would set the wrong expectations for Phase 3.

| | `conveyor belt consultant` (old lead) | `business excellence consultant` (new lead) |
|---|---|---|
| Competitors | A handful of manufacturers; almost no true consultancies | Every management consultancy, every training provider, the Big Four's tail |
| Realistic ceiling | Page one, plausibly top three | Page one is a multi-year content play, not an on-page change |
| Traffic quality | Extremely high intent, very low volume | Higher volume, much broader intent — many searchers are students, job-seekers and researchers |
| What wins it | Being findable at all | Depth, authority, links, and evidence |

**Three consequences, and they change the plan:**

1. **The head term is not the target.** Do not measure success against `business excellence
   consultant`. Target the qualified long tail — method + sector + delivery mode — where a
   two-person specialist can be the best answer on the page.
2. **The conveyor specialism becomes more valuable, not less.** It is now the differentiator *and*
   the cheap entry point: still low-competition, still high-intent, and it feeds the same enquiry
   inbox. Keeping `/specialism` prominent is an SEO decision as much as a positioning one.
3. **Content is the only lever that moves this.** On-page optimisation gets you eligible.
   [Phase 3](02-project-plan.md#phase-3--authority-content--organic-growth) is what gets you ranked.

## 1. How these numbers get validated

**There are no invented search volumes in this document, and there should never be any.**

Search volume and difficulty require a data source we do not have yet. Fabricating them would make
every downstream decision rest on fiction. So this document gives you what *can* be derived without a
paid tool and is more useful at this stage: the keyword universe, the intent behind each term, the
competitive character of each cluster, and a defensible build order.

Fill the volume column when these are in place:

| Source | Gives you | When | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Google Keyword Planner** (free with any Ads account, no spend required) | Volume ranges, related terms, India-specific data | Do this first | Free |
| **Google Search Console** | The queries this site *actually* gets impressions for — the only truly accurate source | 4–8 weeks after Phase 2 | Free |
| **Google autocomplete + "People also ask" + "Related searches"** | Real long-tail phrasing | Anytime | Free |
| **Bing Webmaster Tools keyword research** | Volume estimates without an Ads account | Anytime | Free |
| Ahrefs / Semrush / Ubersuggest | Difficulty, SERP analysis, competitor gaps | When budget allows | Paid |

**Method:** set Keyword Planner's location to India, language English, record the range Google gives
rather than a point estimate, then re-sort the tiers below by *volume ÷ competition*. Record the run
date so a stale figure is visible as stale.

## 4. Build order

Sequenced by **(intent quality × winnability) ÷ effort** — not by volume. A page ranking #3 for a term
20 people search is worth more here than page 4 for a term 2,000 people search.

| Order | What | Why now |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Home page — Clusters A + F | ✅ **Live.** Owns brand terms and the category claim |
| 2 | **`/specialism/conveyor-bulk-handling/`** — Cluster D specialism head | The one page that can rank fast. Highest intent per unit of effort on the whole site |
| 3 | Six service pages — Cluster B | Highest commercial intent; content already exists in the deck |
| 4 | Six industry pages — Cluster E | Very low competition, high intent, cheap to write |
| 5 | Training pages — Cluster C long tail | Bigger audience; needs qualified positioning to be winnable |
| 6 | Cluster-D articles, 2/month | Compounding growth; feeds internal links to 2–5 |
| 7 | Lead magnets | Converts Cluster-D traffic into contacts |
| 8 | Cluster G — local | ⛔ the moment an address exists, this jumps to position 2 |

## 5. Rules for using keywords

1. **Write for the plant engineer or operations manager, then check the keyword.** Never the reverse.
   Google's helpful-content systems demote text assembled around a phrase.
2. **Primary keyword must appear** in the `<title>`, the `<h1>`, the first 100 words, at least one
   `<h2>`, one image `alt`, and the URL slug. That is placement, not repetition.
3. **No keyword density target.** Once it is naturally in the copy, stop.
4. **Every page answers a question a real person asked.** "People also ask" is free primary research.
5. **Internal links use descriptive anchor text** — "lean manufacturing consultant", not "click here".
6. **One page per intent.** If two drafts would say the same thing, they are one page.
7. **Re-run Search Console monthly** and add the queries you already get impressions for but no
   clicks. Fastest wins available, and free to find.
8. **Do not let the category claim erase the specialism.** "Business excellence consultant" is what
   the business calls itself; "conveyor belt consultant" is what gets found. The site needs both, and
   the second is what makes the first believable.
