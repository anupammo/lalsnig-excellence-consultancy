# 08 — Content strategy

How this site earns organic reach: by being the most useful answer available to a specific,
under-served set of questions.

---

## 1. Audience

| Persona | Their trigger | What convinces them | What loses them |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Maintenance head / plant engineer** — the searcher | A belt that keeps failing; a downtime figure they have to explain | Diagnostic specificity. Naming the failure mode they are looking at | Marketing language, vague "solutions", no technical depth |
| **Operations / plant manager** — the budget | Throughput or cost pressure; an audit finding | Quantified outcomes, a defined process, low disruption | No evidence, no method, no idea what it costs |
| **HR / L&D manager** — the training buyer | A skills gap, a certification requirement, an annual training plan | Programme structure, trainer credentials, on-site delivery | Generic course catalogues indistinguishable from ten others |
| **Procurement** — the gatekeeper | An approved-vendor process | Registration details, references, insurance, compliance | Anything that looks like a one-man operation with no paperwork |

The engineer finds the site. The manager approves it. Content has to satisfy both without patronising
either — technical depth up front, business consequence stated plainly alongside it.

## 2. Voice

**Specific, plain, and never overclaiming.** The brand's own line is the standard:
*"We don't just recommend improvement — we build the capability to sustain it."*

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "Off-centre loading at the transfer chute" | "Suboptimal material presentation" |
| "18+ years in conveyor belt manufacturing" | "Decades of unparalleled expertise" |
| "Reduces spillage and the clean-up labour that follows it" | "Drives transformational value" |
| Name the failure mode | Describe the benefit and skip the mechanism |
| Say what you don't know | Imply omniscience |
| Indian English, `en-IN` spelling | Mixed US/UK spelling in the same paragraph |

**Every claim must be defensible.** No invented client counts, no invented percentages, no
"trusted by leading companies" without naming one. A specialist's credibility is destroyed faster by
one unsupportable number than it is built by ten good ones.

## 3. Content pillars

Three topic clusters. Each pillar page is comprehensive; each cluster article answers one question and
links back up.

### Pillar 1 — Conveyor belt troubleshooting *(the traffic engine)*

The highest-volume problem-led cluster and the one where this business's expertise is least
substitutable.

- Belt mistracking: causes and systematic correction
- Spillage and carry-back: transfer point and cleaning system fixes
- Splice and joint failure modes
- Idler and pulley failure analysis
- Cover wear, gouging and impact damage
- Steel cord vs. textile belting: selection guide

### Pillar 2 — Conveyor maintenance & reliability *(the commercial engine)*

Reaches the reader who has budget and a reliability target.

- Building a conveyor preventive-maintenance schedule
- Inspection routes and condition checklists
- Critical spares strategy for belt conveyors
- Calculating the true cost of conveyor downtime
- Extending belt life: the levers that actually move
- Conveyor safety audit checklist

### Pillar 3 — Operational excellence in bulk handling *(the training engine)*

Bridges the conveyor identity into the Lean/Six Sigma offer, which is where the larger audience is.

- Applying Lean Six Sigma to maintenance processes
- Value stream mapping a material-handling operation
- 5S on the maintenance floor
- Time and motion study in plant operations
- Building an operator competency matrix
- Using Power BI for plant KPI dashboards

## 4. Content types and their job

| Type | Job | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| Service page | Convert commercial intent | Once, maintained |
| Industry page | Capture sector-qualified searches | Once, maintained |
| Pillar page | Own a topic; anchor a cluster | 3 total |
| Cluster article | Capture long-tail; feed the pillar | 2/month |
| **Case study** | **The single strongest credibility asset** | As permission allows |
| Lead magnet (checklist, diagnostic table) | Convert traffic into contacts | 3 total |
| FAQ | Capture question queries; pre-empt objections | Per page |

> **Case studies matter more than everything else on this list.** Two paragraphs of *"belt at a cement
> plant was failing every eleven weeks; the cause was X; after the change it ran twenty-nine weeks"*
> outperforms twenty pages of capability description. The only blocker is client permission — and
> anonymisation ("a cement plant in eastern India") solves most of it. Pursue this in Phase 2.

## 5. Distribution & organic reach

Publishing is not distribution. Each of these is a channel that already exists and is under-used.

| Channel | Play |
|---|---|
| **LinkedIn** — the strongest existing asset | Ranjit Mondal already has a professional network in exactly this industry. Every article gets a native LinkedIn post — the substance in the post itself, the link in the first comment (native content out-reaches link posts). Cadence: 2/week |
| **Google Search** | The compounding channel. Everything in [06](06-keyword-research.md) and [07](07-seo-onpage-spec.md) serves this |
| **Google Business Profile** | Highest-ROI local channel available to a services business. ⛔ blocked on the address |
| **Industry associations & directories** | Bulk-handling and materials-handling bodies, engineering directories. Links plus qualified referral traffic |
| **Guest articles** | Indian manufacturing and mining publications. The strongest available backlink source |
| **Training-provider listings** | Where L&D managers actually look for on-site training |
| **Email** | Only once there is a list, and only with something worth sending. A list of thirty engaged plant engineers beats three thousand scraped addresses |

### Reach levers specific to this business

1. **Answer the question in the first paragraph.** AI overviews and featured snippets both reward it, and so do readers.
2. **Publish the diagnostic tables.** A symptom → cause → check → fix table for belt mistracking is the kind of asset people bookmark and link to. Bookmarks and links are the ranking signal.
3. **Use real photographs of real work** the moment they exist. Stock imagery is a ceiling on trust.
4. **Name the standards** — ISO 9001, CEMA, DIN 22101, IS 11592. It signals competence to engineers and gives search engines topical entities to attach to.
5. **Update rather than republish.** A page refreshed with a visible `dateModified` beats a near-duplicate new post that cannibalises it.

## 6. Editorial calendar (Phase 3)

Two articles a month. **The bottleneck is SME review time, not writing time** — plan around Ranjit's
and Ruturaj's availability, not around a content quota.

| Month | Article 1 | Article 2 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pillar: Conveyor belt troubleshooting | Belt mistracking: causes and fixes |
| 2 | Reducing spillage and carry-back | Steel cord vs. textile belting |
| 3 | Pillar: Conveyor maintenance & reliability | Building a PM schedule + **checklist lead magnet** |
| 4 | The true cost of conveyor downtime | Splice failure modes and prevention |
| 5 | Conveyor safety audit checklist | Idler and pulley failure analysis |
| 6 | Pillar: Operational excellence in bulk handling | Lean Six Sigma applied to maintenance |

Every article: SME review before publish, `Article` schema, links up to its pillar and sideways to two
siblings, one relevant service-page CTA, and a `dateModified` refresh whenever the content changes.

## 7. Governance

- **Nothing technical publishes without SME sign-off.** A wrong belt-tension formula costs more credibility than a missed publishing date.
- **Review cadence:** every article revisited at 12 months — update, merge, or retire.
- **No AI-generated content published unreviewed.** Drafting assistance is fine; publishing without an expert pass is not, and it is exactly what Google's helpful-content systems are built to detect.
- **One owner per page**, recorded in the checklist.
